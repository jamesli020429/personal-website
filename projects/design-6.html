PAVING WITH BOOKS

Book Building Complex of Beijing

This project integrates functional planning with the reconstruction of urban public space systems through architectural design. Located in Xidan's commercial district, Beijing Book Building is in urgent need of renewal. By separating the fixed and flexible functions of a typical library, this proposal utilizes fixed programs to shape a three-dimensional landscape path. This path enhances the public space system while creating a year-round comfortable indoor public courtyard.

Location: Beijing, China

Tutor: Martijn De Geus

Time: June, 2023

Site: Commercial District and Urban Axis

Located next to the only large commercial district in Xicheng, Beijing, the site is not only facing the urban axis of Chang'An street, but also back to back with the residential courtyards (Hutong). Therefore it is crucial to evaluate the building's shading to nearby areas as well as the main facade facing north and south.

Strategy: Separating Fixed / Flexible Functions

The original building suffers from mixed internal functions that often interfere with one another. By reorganizing and separating fixed and flexible functions, I designed a 3D public path to link scattered public spaces. This path directly integrates with outdoor routes, creating a seamless, continuous, and comfortable leisure space for the public.

Responding to Site

According to the flow of people, I responds to the circular elements of the adjacent mall, our layout of topography and landscape naturally introduces people from all directions. After that, the public path is paved onto the site three-dimensionally, where different types of fixed functions like reading rooms, archives, and offices are inserted inside. At last, the massing is wrapped in a glass box to define boundaries of the building, as well as responding to the surrounding building elevations facing Chang’An Avenue.

Interior Experience

Central Atrium

In the central atrium, the dislocation and offset of floors create a sense of embrace, enhanced by greenery such as ivy and acacia arranged linearly along the edges. Visitors can rest and read in this garden-like space, immersed in the gentleness of nature.

The Stairway

In the stairway, the flexible use of curtains allows for diverse activities, transforming the space into smaller areas of varying sizes and scales for independent use. Scattered plants along the stairway guide movement upward, adding a dynamic touch to the journey.

The Frames

As the path ascends, strategically placed windows frame views of nearby historical landmarks. From the eastern window, the Forbidden City glimmers in the setting sun, its golden glazed tiles glowing. From the northern window, the hutongs appear nestled among the green trees, offering a glimpse of Beijing’s rich history and culture.

Sustainability

Adjustable Roof

To enhance comfort within the building, I incorporated a seesaw roof design for skylights, complemented by solar panels on the south side. This arrangement not only conserves energy but also provides effective shading.

Double Skin Facade

A ventilation gap is created between the inner and outer skins of the building. The inner skin features operable windows that facilitate indoor air circulation. Additionally, the floors are partially cantilevered with hollow sections in the middle, serving as standing platforms for glass cleaning staff while maintaining uninterrupted airflow.
