THE HUB

Urban Design of New Student Center

This project focuses on urban regeneration through transportation-oriented design. With the upcoming construction of a metro exchange station adjacent to our campus, a new student center is proposed, integrating transportation, student amenities, and museums within a single complex. This center aims to not only create a modern identity for the campus but also to revitalize the surrounding student communities, enriching urban life in the area.

Location: Beijing, China

Tutor: Martijn De Geus

Time: November, 2023

Site Analysis

Traffic Congestions

Tsinghua's campus layout cut off a major route connecting downtown Beijing to Changping District. The narrow road skirting the campus, allowing only single-lane traffic, will face increased congestion with the construction of the new east gate station. This design must address this critical issue.

Clashing Functions

The proposed station site is at the intersection of multiple functions: a tourist center for the newly built art and science museums, while also serving as a primary route for students living off-campus heading to the university.

Ambiguous Edges

Though the museums are technically part of the campus, their boundaries with the public remain unclear. The public-private relationship between the museums, campus, and the surrounding area needs clearer definition.

Flow Generation

Radical Pragmatism

Inspired by OMA’s Euralille project, my proposal employs “radical pragmatism” to solve traffic issues by reconnecting the G7 highway underground and the originally narrow Heqing Road, linking them directly to the main road to downtown Beijing. Along with the creation of better public space, this approach transforms the new east gate of Tsinghua into a highly connected and inclusive student center.

Taking Responsibility

As a Tsinghua University student, I feel a responsibility to not only address the traffic congestion caused by poor campus planning but also to enhance public access to the museums and campus culture, benefiting the surrounding student community.

New East Gate Station: A New Impression

The new East Gate station will integrate the existing underground Line 15 with a new light rail Line 13 station. As the core of the new transportation hub, it connects directly to the new east gate via a dedicated bicycle trail and provides ample space for cars, bikes, and pedestrians. The station also features an alumni center on the top floor and a rooftop garden.

The Underground Hub: Urban Life Reimagined

Given the tight planning and narrow streets in Beijing, utilising underground construction could offer a solution for improving pedestrian spaces. By incorporating shopfronts, greenery, and sound-cancelling walls, these underground areas can feel more enclosed and comfortable for pedestrians.
